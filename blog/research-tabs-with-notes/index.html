How to keep your research notes and sources before closing Chrome tabs

Before you close your research tabs, write down what you decided or what you still need to check. Attach a screenshot of the detail that matters and save the few source links you’ll need to reopen.

By the Alt+Q Notes team · November 25, 2026 · Workflow for Alt+Q Notes 0.4.2

Keep the reason you opened the tabs

Imagine you’re comparing clamp lamps for a studio desk. You have the product page, a dimension drawing, and a review open. Tomorrow you’ll want to know why this lamp was on your shortlist and what to check before buying it.

Example note

Shortlist the clamp lamp. The drawing says the clamp opens to 42 mm. Measure the desk edge, then check whether the arm reaches the center of the drawing board.

  • Screenshot. Keep the part of the dimension drawing that shows the 42 mm measurement.
  • Source links. Save the product page for the model, the drawing for the measurement, and the review for the arm’s reach.

Save the note, then attach the source tabs

  1. Press Alt + Q on Windows or Option + Q on Mac. Write the decision and the next thing to check. Paste the screenshot and click Save.
  2. Open that note for editing in the Alt+Q Notes side panel. Hold Ctrl on Windows or ⌘ on Mac and select the source tabs in Chrome’s tab strip.
  3. Click Add 3 selected tabs and check the links in the note. Click Save to keep their titles and URLs. The selected tabs you attached then close.

See the buttons and shortcuts in the 30-second guide to saving a screenshot and tabs in Chrome.

Reopen the source you need

When you return, open the note in the side panel. If the desk edge measures 45 mm, the screenshot lets you rule out this lamp. If you need to verify the drawing or find another size, click its saved link to reopen the source.

Saved links open the current version of each page. Keep a screenshot of any detail you’ll need even if the source changes.
