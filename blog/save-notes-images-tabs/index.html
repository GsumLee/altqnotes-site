How to save a note, screenshot, and tabs in Chrome

To save a screenshot and source tabs with a note in Chrome, paste the image into a quick note and save it. Then open the note in the Alt+Q Notes side panel and add the tabs you want to revisit.

By the Alt+Q Notes team · Tested on 0.4.2 in desktop Chromium on Windows

1. Save a quick note and screenshot

Open any regular webpage. Press Alt + Q on Windows or Option + Q on Mac. If you’re using Alt+Q Notes for the first time, review the first-run notice and finish or skip the tutorial.

Write a line you’ll understand tomorrow. In the demo it’s “Shortlist the oak chair for the reading nook.” Copy an image or screenshot to the clipboard, click inside the note, then press Ctrl + V (⌘ + V on Mac). Check the thumbnail and click Save.

2. Add the tabs you compared

Open Alt+Q Notes from the Chrome toolbar to show its side panel. Open the saved note for editing. In Chrome’s tab strip, hold Ctrl on Windows or ⌘ on Mac and select at least two tabs. In the editor, click Add 2 selected tabs if you selected two. Allow tab access if Chrome asks, then click Save.

Saving closes the selected tabs you attached after their links and titles are saved.

3. Reopen a link from the note

Next time, open the side panel and find your note. Its thumbnail keeps the screenshot in view, and clicking a saved tab link opens that page in Chrome.

Common questions

Do I need a Google account to save notes?

You can save notes, images, and tab links in your Chrome profile without a Google account. Connect a Google account if you choose to use Google Drive sync.

What does a saved tab link reopen?

It reopens the saved URL. A tab attachment stores the page title and URL. Paste a screenshot into the note if you want to keep a detail from the page as it looked at the time.
